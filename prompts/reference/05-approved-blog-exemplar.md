## What Is Cost Per Acquisition (CPA) for Insurance Agents?

Cost per acquisition — also called cost to acquire a client — is the total amount you spent to close one paying client.

The formula is stupid simple:

**CPA = Total spend ÷ Clients closed**

That's it.

Buy $1,000 in leads, close 4 clients → your CPA is $250.

Now pair it with the other number that matters: **collected per sale (CPS)** — the average revenue you actually collect from each closed client.

Those two numbers give you your **return multiple**:

**Return multiple = Total collected ÷ Total spend**

CPA of $333… average collected of a few thousand per sale… that's how the "angry" agent above landed at 10x.

And once you know your return multiple, something powerful happens:

You stop reacting to lead packs emotionally…

And you start running your book like a business.

## How to Calculate Your Cost to Acquire a Client (3 Numbers)

You don't need fancy software. You need three numbers, tracked on EVERY batch of leads you buy:

**#1 Total spend.**

Everything that went into the batch — lead cost, processing fees, platform fees. All of it.

**#2 Clients closed.**

Actual paying clients. Not booked appointments. Not "great conversations." Closed.

**#3 Total collected.**

Real collected revenue — not the annualized premium number that looks pretty on paper.

Then run the two formulas:

- CPA = Total spend ÷ Clients closed
- Return multiple = Total collected ÷ Total spend

Do this on every batch. Not once. EVERY batch.

## FAQ: Cost Per Acquisition for Insurance Agents

**What's the difference between cost per lead and cost per acquisition?**
Cost per lead is what one lead costs upfront. Cost per acquisition is your total spend divided by clients actually CLOSED — the number that tells you whether your marketing is profitable.

**Why is cost per lead considered almost irrelevant?**
Because a cheap lead that never converts produces a worse CPA than a pricier lead that closes reliably. Optimizing for CPL alone is optimizing three steps too early in the funnel.

**How do I calculate my cost to acquire a client?**
Total spend on a batch ÷ clients closed from that batch. Then compare total collected to total spend for your return multiple.

**Should I switch lead vendors if one pack underperforms?**
Not automatically. Check CPA across multiple packs first. One soft batch is usually normal variance — the agent in this article was complaining about a 10x return. If CPA stays broken across several batches, that's when you act.
