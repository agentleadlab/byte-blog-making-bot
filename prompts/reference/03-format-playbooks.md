# Matthew Volkwyn — Format Playbooks

**Purpose:** Step-by-step build instructions for each deliverable type, reverse-engineered from MV's actual published work. Follow the anatomy, then apply the Voice Profile (doc 01) and Systems (doc 02).

---

## PLAYBOOK 1 — Nurture / Education Email

**Observed length:** 400–900 words. **Delivery format:** SL (subject line) + PT (preview text) stated at top, then body.

**Note:** MV's list emails come in three recurring types — Education (this playbook), YouTube/content-promo (use the **Noteworthy Format, System 61** in Doc 02), and Promo (Playbook 2). Label which type you're writing.

**Anatomy:**
1. **SL + PT** — built with BCD-P + Eye Magnets (write 5–7, deliver 2–3). PT extends or qualifies the SL ("- for those making 25k/mo and beyond").
2. **Hook line** — first sentence includes the reader's [FirstName] and a curiosity/benefit/drama payload. Often a confession or discovery frame: "I only realized this last month Marc…"
3. **Story or scene** — a real person (mentee, client, friend, or MV himself) with specific numbers, month/year, and place. Prefer dialogue entry or time-and-place entry (Story Lead rules). 
4. **The turn** — a blunt bolded truth aimed at the reader: "Sorry to break it to you Marc… If you don't have an assistant, you're your own assistant."
5. **MV's own proof** — his results relevant to the lesson, woven not dumped (Your Words rules).
6. **The teach** — numbered framework (#1…#5), each with a bold header line and 1–3 short lines of explanation. Optionally: "In Friday's email, I'll cover #2…" (open loop to next send).
7. **Challenge questions** — 2–4 consecutive direct questions escalating in discomfort ("How long will you be okay with it? … When will you get OBSESSED?"). Then release: "Some hard questions to ask yourself."
8. **Sign-off:** "In your corner," + MV / Matthew / Matthew Volkwyn.
9. **P.S. = the pitch — always topically bridged.** The P.S. never sells the offer generically; it sells the offer's benefit that's specific to THIS email's topic (delegation email → "In the Dojo we have a talent pool of trusted assistants"; income email → the coaching that got mentees to $X/mo). Pattern: timely reason → offer → topical benefit → qualification + price honesty ("It's not cheap. But it's the fastest path I know of.") → CTA ("DM '1-1'" / "Reply 'DOJO'") → proof line in parentheses. Sometimes an explicit anti-pitch: "I don't want to make this email into a pitch… but if you want my help, just reply and lmk."

**Multi-part series mechanics:** When an email is part N of a series: (1) open with a fresh self-deprecating hook, NOT a recap ("The first assistant I ever hired, I asked her to buy books for me on Amazon…… I tried to delegate it! And I thought I was a leader. 🤣"); (2) re-teach the core reframe briefly; (3) THEN recap: "But first, a quick recap. Yesterday I shared the 5 steps… We covered step 1 yesterday."; (4) restate the full numbered list; (5) continue with the next step(s); (6) close the loop forward if more parts remain ("In Friday's email, I'll cover…").

**Embedded swipe assets:** MV includes complete, usable templates inside emails — a full sample job description, a word-for-word upsell message, a testimonial-request script — followed by an honesty caveat: "Okay, so that's a little short obviously. If this were real, I'd flesh it out more. But that's the 80/20." Give the reader something they can copy-paste today.

**Third-party attribution:** He credits others' frameworks by name without ego ("I follow Taki Moore's simple 'ME WE YOU' delegation process," Hormozi quotes, "as Chris Haddad told me"). Borrowed frameworks get named sources; his own get ™-style branded names.

**Aphorism repetition:** Key maxims repeat verbatim within one email for drilling effect ("Hire slow, fire fast." appears twice). One aphorism per email max gets this treatment.

## PLAYBOOK 2 — Promo / Launch Email Sequence

**Observed arc (7–9 emails over ~6 days):**
1. **Warning/Trend email** — the world is changing; story proof of someone left behind; trend-cycle timeline; soft P.S. pitch.
2. **Unfairness email** — the game is rigged (nepotism/referrals story with real dollar figures); "Don't hate the player. Hate the game."; reframe: "Who do I need to know?"; P.S. origin story of the offer.
3. **Good-news/Opportunity email** — numbered reasons (#1–#3, each bolded) why NOW favors the reader; "perfect storm" framing; P.S. urgency (price raise, seat count).
4. **Proof-avalanche email** — open by attacking fake guru testimonials, then "That's why I ALWAYS bring receipts"; long named list of member results including the imperfect ones; event/community invitation; CTA.
5. **Short-n-sweet scarcity email** — 60–150 words; one idea ("You are the average of the five people…"); seat count; reply CTA; sharp P.S. ("If your network doesn't make you wake up feeling broke… you're probably anchored to the rocks.").
6. **48-hour email** — ultra-short; deadline + seats + CTA; P.S. flex ("Mentees who secured seats in 2021 still hold them. Let that sink in.").
7. **24-hour email** — "If you can't beat em' join em'"; FOMO forecast ("You're going to see win after win… and you could've joined for less"); P.P.S. that breaks the fourth wall with sincerity: "whether you join us or not, make 2026 your best year. I believe in you."
8. **Final-chance email** — "Last LAST FINAL Chance"; self-aware ("my last shameless promo email"); one-conversation-away story; Hard Way vs. Rocketship (Two-Paths); price-of-ignorance math; Hormozi "pay in time or money"; named recent wins; CTA; P.S. deadline; P.P.S. statistical joke ("You're 3x more likely to get divorced TWICE than fail with us").

**Sequence rules:** every email standalone-readable; urgency always real (dates, counts); CTA is a reply keyword ("DOJO") leading to a qualification chat, not a checkout link; alternate long story emails with short scarcity emails; always include at least one moment of sincerity that breaks the promo script.

**Reminder & urgency email builds (System 76):** For product/checkout promos (Copy Systems™, client offers), use the two formulas: **Reminder Email** = intriguing lead → "didn't want you to miss this" → marketing message → CTA → 3 benefit bullets → urgency injection → urgency CTA → P.S. mechanism + proof. **Urgency Email** (final sends) = deadline fact first → marketing message → benefits → scarcity CTA → benefits reframed → scarcity CTA → take-away selling. Always give urgency a mundane reason-why (stock delay, schedule quirk) and human texture ("we're off to dinner", "I'll shut it down after I wake up and ice bath") — casual honesty about the deadline beats theatrical precision.

## PLAYBOOK 3 — Facebook Post

**Observed length:** 100–700 words. **No hashtags. Minimal links (link "below in the comments" when promoting YouTube).**

**Variants:**

**A. Win/Celebration post** (most common):
1. Hook line — aphorism, hot take, or shock: "Your new life is going to cost you your old one." / "STOP… I can't keep up with this copywriter's wins anymore 😭"
2. The win — anonymous-but-specific mentee ("This copywriter…") + exact numbers and timeline.
3. "How?" / "Here's his sEcReT." — the mechanism reveal.
4. The principle — the transferable lesson, 3–8 short lines.
5. Reader application — challenge or mini-checklist.
6. Warm close — congratulate the person by tag or "Congrats bro 🔥"; sometimes "- MV".
7. (Production note: screenshot attachment referenced.)

**B. Teaching/List post:**
1. Hook (contrarian): "One of the fastest ways to grow your income as a copywriter is by firing your clients."
2. Proof story with numbers.
3. Numbered framework (1–5), each with a header thought + short elaboration + honest caveats.
4. Blunt closer: "And if you don't see a future, pull the plug. Thank me later. - MV"

**C. Hot-take post:**
1. "Hot take - …" or a provocative claim ("$25k/mo is the new 6-figures.")
2. Reasoned case with stats and examples.
3. A "please please please" plea or challenge.
4. Proof points; direct instruction to act today.

**D. Interview-promo post:**
1. Question hook tied to the guest's expertise.
2. A TEENY sneak-peek insight (genuinely useful, incomplete).
3. Guest credibility.
4. Bulleted "He also covers:" (3–4 curiosity bullets, benefit-loaded, no reveals).
5. "Link below in the comments."

**E. Personal-story post (rare, high-impact):**
Fake-out joke hook ("Here's what cleaning toilets taught me about B2B sales… KIDDING 🤣 But I did mop a lot of toilets…") → vivid scene with sensory detail → the decision moment → time-jump contrast ("8 years later, I'm writing this from…") → numbered principles (#1 Refusing mediocrity #2 Paying the price) → universalize: "If a dude who worked at Tim Hortons for minimum wage could… you can too."

**F. Extended-metaphor teaching post:**
1. Hook = the metaphor as a joke claim: "If you want to consistently produce winners as a creative strategist, you need to become a professional GOLD DIGGER 🤣 / Seriously…"
2. Anchor with a mentee win + numbers ("got a $3,000 bonus from a client before he sent his first invoice").
3. "How?" → map the metaphor element-by-element to the real process (miners looked for evidence of gold before digging → check winning ads in the account, from competitors, from adjacent niches).
4. The doctrine line ("winning ads are almost NEVER new — they're usually an iteration of something already working").
5. Personal-arc close: "A few years ago I didn't enjoy this work… But now? I genuinely love this space. Haha"
6. Screenshot attached.

**G. Proof/authority post (Dojo results):**
1. Open by quoting the doubters with emoji reaction tags: "'Copywriting is dead!!!!' 💀 / 'It's a commodity.' 😁 / 'You can't make 6 figures anymore.' 🤡 / Ha Ha."
2. Reverse psychology: "Good. Please believe that. That way it's even easier for all of us…"
3. Fresh named win with before/after numbers + longevity claim ("4 years coaching… we don't recycle our testimonials").
4. Honest success-rate framing including the caveat ("those who haven't achieved success yet… we coach them until they do. No one left behind.").
5. Numbered reasons WHY the results happen (small + high quality, only takes fits, "I'M STILL IN THE TRENCHES" + a current personal deal, systems updated constantly).
6. Challenge-and-wait flex: "Show me where else… I'll wait. 😁"
7. Gratitude close to clients ("You make coaching so much easier 🏆🙏").
8. P.S. = live seat count as urgency: "One spot was taken today - so now we're at 99/100."

**H. Instagram variant:** Same voice, compressed — 8–15 short lines, one story beat (the mastermind of 60 entrepreneurs), one CAPS-drilled principle ("They REFUSE to accept the price."), a "take it from dozens of millionaires" authority line, one-line command close. No links, no bullets, minimal emoji.

**Post craft rules (all variants):** seasonal/timely angles when available ("It's summer… people are posting about their lives — more natural conversation openers right now"); etiquette clauses that protect the reader from misusing the advice ("Just don't be the person who turns a connection call into a pitch. If business comes up naturally, great. But schedule a separate call for it."); permission lines that lower the bar ("You don't need to be at $20k/month to be worth talking to."); honest retractions when teaching from old stories ("This story was from 2021… I do NOT recommend outreach now. But the principle stands."); and nuance-guard P.S. after hot takes ("That's not what I'm talking about. I'm talking about…").

## PLAYBOOK 4 — Long-Form Sales Letter

**Observed anatomy (from the Copy Systems™ letter):**
1. **Pre-head:** URGENT WARNING label.
2. **Headline:** enemy/competitor frame + big specific claim + open loop ("Keep Reading To Discover How They Do It… 👇").
3. **"From The ~~Desk~~ Penthouse of…"** — playful strikethrough status joke.
4. **Name-drop wall** — alternating-bold client list + "…plus HUNDREDS more (no exaggeration)."
5. **False-secret dismissal → thesis:** "Your competitors don't have some secret strategy… it's simpler than that" → bolded mechanism thesis (systems).
6. **Origin failure story** — humiliation scene with dialogue, time pressure (⏰ tik tok), public embarrassment quote, consequence. Emotional low point fully felt.
7. **The discovery/build montage** — obsession sequence ("Every line… I dissected. Every trigger… I documented.").
8. **The mechanism** — what the product IS, as a 4-step usage loop.
9. **Transformation proof** — MV's results, then mentee results (named, numbered).
10. **Offer reveal + "unfair advantage" benefits** (bulleted outcomes).
11. **Use-case navigation** ("Need to write a VSL lead? → System #6").
12. **Testimonial block** with quantified stories.
13. **Price anchoring** — $1,000/hr history, $15k consulting ROI story → "Not for $1,000. Not even for $500. → $97/month → coffee-per-day math."
14. **"Why so cheap?" objection section** — numbered reasons mixing altruism with self-aware selfishness ("(Sneaky) Reason #4: I can roll in some paydays as the greedy capitalist that I am. 😉").
15. **Guarantee + repulsion clause** — 30-day unconditional, BUT "Once you cancel or refund, you cannot rejoin. Dead serious." + wealth qualifier.
16. **Bonuses** (#1 live critique call, #2 AI secrets) — each with its own mini-pitch.
17. **Deadline** — doors close date, price rises on reopen.
18. **Two-Paths close** → "Choose wisely." → CTA → final deadline sting.
19. **P.S. for skimmers** — "HEY You pesky skimmer" → TLDR link. **P.P.S.** → full bullet stack (see Playbook 5) → FAQ.
20. **FAQ with attitude** — answers that qualify, tease, and joke ("No. BAD. This is another beginner question.").

## PLAYBOOK 5 — "Death By Bullet" / TLDR Page

For skimmers and offer-aware traffic:
1. Self-aware header: "So You Wanna Skip Straight To The Good Stuff, Eh?"
2. One-paragraph what-it-is + name-drop wall.
3. **Spec-sheet block:** PRICE (anchor joke first: "$5000 — SERIOUSLY!? No, just price-anchoring you 🤡🤣"), ACTUAL PRICE, CANCELLATION POLICY, DUE DATE, WHAT YOU GET, WHO IT'S FOR. CTA link.
4. **The bullet stack** — grouped by module/section, each section with: range label (Systems #10–20), THEME NAME, one-line promise, then ✅ bullets.
5. **Bullet construction rules (his actual craft):**
   - Named proprietary mechanisms ("The Poo-Flinging Monkey Technique," "TEA Framework," "Curiously Familiar tactic")
   - Specific results embedded ("took an email list from $15k/mo to $200k/mo")
   - Self-referential jokes ("bullet boosters that suck prospects into the next bullet. Kinda like I sucked you into this bullet. I GOT YOU!")
   - Forbidden-knowledge teases ("the one Welcome Email I might get sued for breaking down. IYKYK. 😈")
   - Contrarian openers ("FACT: 7% of the market spends 70% of the money…")
   - System-number references [System #12] for tangibility
   - Every group ends "✅ PLUS SO MUCH MORE…"
6. Mid-stack CTA break ("Btw, we're only HALF WAY… if you're ready, click here").
7. Save the best category for last, labeled as such.
8. Recurring-value close ("We're ADDING new systems Every. Single. Month.") + final CTA.

## PLAYBOOK 6 — Ad Copy (from System 3 + observed patterns)

1. **Hook = I-BCD.** Identifier(s) in the FIRST line, always. Benefit stated fast. Then curiosity/drama.
2. **Lead** — pick from the 7 Leads; Proclamation and Story leads dominate his ad examples; hook first, story starts line two.
3. **Body** — problem agitation with shared-experience identifiers; mechanism tease (Curiously Familiar name when appropriate); credibility woven BEFORE claims.
4. **Close** — clear single CTA; "WITHOUT X" stacking near the CTA ("No cold-emailing. No DM'ing strangers. No begging for referrals. Just a calendar booked solid…").
5. **Compliance sensibility** — prefer process-focused hooks over claim hooks in health/finance ("This is what happens to your gut if you take one cup daily for four weeks" beats "Heal your gut in 4 weeks" — educational feel, imagination does the selling, no flaggable claim).

## PLAYBOOK 7 — Advertorial (synthesized from his ad + story systems)

MV has no dedicated advertorial sample in the source set; build advertorials as **Proclamation or Story lead → long-form educational body → Offer lead close**, using: journalistic authority framing (studies, reports, screenshots), Curiously Familiar mechanism naming, character mirroring through a relatable protagonist, credibility before every claim, and a soft product reveal two-thirds in. Flag to the user that this format is extrapolated, and offer extra revision passes.

## PLAYBOOK 8 — YouTube Video Script

**From his published videos. Loose, spoken register (see Voice Profile §8) — script should read like talk, not prose.**

1. **Cold open = the promise, zero preamble:** "Okay, so if I just want [OUTCOME] completely from scratch… here's exactly what I do step-by-step." Hook before intro, always.
2. **Compressed intro:** name + 2 credibility facts (copywriters coached, client revenue) + one self-aware environment joke ("I'm wearing sunglasses because the sun is in my eyes and I don't like it. Okay, you know everything about me. Let's get started.").
3. **Lead magnet mention early:** "I've built a guide/checklist that comes with this video" — widen the audience ("this isn't just for beginners; if you're at $3–10k/mo, do this too").
4. **Numbered steps**, each with: the instruction → the why (industry context, what changed) → a script/example of exactly what to say or do → a story from his own career (including mistakes: "I bought a $300 suit… I didn't realize the industry doesn't need a suit") → the principle.
5. **Physical/sport analogies** to land principles (barefoot race, flippers, marathon, Ronaldo's penalty).
6. **Environment asides as pacing:** narrate the shoot conditions ("the sun is eating up this light, let me wrap this up") — authenticity beats polish.
7. **Mid-video soft CTA** framed as accountability, not selling: "If you want me to shake you up a little every week, join the email list."
8. **Cross-reference his own videos** ("I've got a video on systems — watch that and you'll get what I mean").
9. **Close:** recap in one breath, point to description resources (pricing guide etc.), then casual exit: "Now I'm going to go hang out with the Dojo. I'll chat with you in the next video. Take care."

## UNIVERSAL PRE-FLIGHT CHECKLIST (run before delivering ANY piece)

1. ☐ NRO stated in one sentence before drafting
2. ☐ Hook scored on BCD (or I-BCD for ads; BCD-P for subject lines)
3. ☐ Lead type consciously chosen from the 7 (name which one)
4. ☐ Identifiers present (ads/social especially)
5. ☐ Credibility placed BEFORE major claims
6. ☐ Specific numbers, names, dates (no vague claims) — placeholders like [RESULT/$X/MONTH] where real facts are needed from the client
7. ☐ Urgency (if any) is real, never manufactured
8. ☐ Qualification/repulsion present in offers
9. ☐ One-sentence paragraphs; ellipses; caps/bold per Voice Profile
10. ☐ At least one moment of humor AND one moment of sincerity
11. ☐ Read-aloud test: does it sound like a voice note from MV?
12. ☐ QBC edit pass: "So what? Can this be shorter? Why does this matter?"
