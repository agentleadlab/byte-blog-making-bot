# Matthew Volkwyn — Copy Systems & Frameworks Reference

**Purpose:** The operating system behind MV's copy. Part A distills his documented Systems 1–9 (Art of Attention: hooks, subject lines, ad hooks, leads, credibility). Part B contains additional named frameworks from his materials, including System 76 (Urgency & Reminders) and his client-acquisition roadmap. Every piece of copy produced in this Project should be traceable to these systems.

# PART A — THE DOCUMENTED SYSTEMS (1–9)

## System 1 — Hooks (The BCD Formula)

The hook is the first impression: subject line + preview text in email, first lines in ads, first seconds in video. If the hook fails, everything else is wasted. Clients judge copy by hooks too.

**Process:**
1. **Market Immersion™ first** — not "research." Absorb the market's language the way you'd absorb chess lingo by hanging around chess players. Use the reader's own words.
2. **Write about what the market cares about.** Enter the conversation already happening in their head (WIIFM — "why should I care?"). Sell the destination, not the airplane food.
3. **Apply BCD — Benefit, Curiosity, Drama:**
   - **Benefit** = the outcome they want, ideally specific ("Shed 20 pounds in 30 days without giving up your favorite foods")
   - **Curiosity** = incomplete information; the mind demanding closure ("Why cardio will make you fat")
   - **Drama** = human stakes, story, shock; the heart on the edge of its seat ("My boss fired me after he saw this…")
   - Curiosity = documentary. Drama = psychological thriller. Combinations beat singles.
4. **Edit hooks with interrogation questions:** Is this useful? Can it be shorter? More curiosity? More specific benefit (numbers/data)? More drama?
5. **Write multiple hooks per piece and test.** The market decides, not you.

Progression example: "Make money with Amazon" (vague) → "Brand new way to make money on Amazon" (better) → "A New Way to Earn on Amazon Without Selling Physical Products" (working).

## System 2 — Email Subject Lines (BCD-P + Eye Magnets)

**Context checks before copy gets blamed:** (1) Tech/deliverability — bad ESP setup caps open rates regardless of copy; (2) List source — organic > paid > cross-promo > purchased; (3) List size — open rates are relative; compare against the list's own history, never against other businesses.

**BCD-P:** BCD plus **Personality** — email subscribers know you, so personal subject lines work ("I got 3.1 million impressions", "I almost died…").

**The 8 Eye Magnets** (pattern interrupts for crowded inboxes):
1. **Numbers** — "5 - 1 = the secret to success?", "34%"
2. **Wacky words** — "Rekt.", "Nani!", "Lekker bru"
3. **Shocking statements** — drama to the extreme; use carefully
4. **Credibility claims** — familiar names, brands, reports ("Elon Musk: Skills > College degrees")
5. **Dialogue/quotes** — "'Shouldn't you be dead?'", "'Am I a failure?'"
6. **FOMO** — "2 seats left.", "Sorry it's over."
7. **First name** — highest-attention word a person knows; don't overuse
8. **Emojis** — last resort only

**Volume rule:** Write 5–7 subject lines per email. Score each against BCD-P (rate B, C, D out of 10; can personality sneak in?). Deliver/test the best 2–3. A/B test always.

## System 3 — Ad Hooks (The I-BCD Formula)

Ads add **Identifiers** to BCD. Identifiers make the IDEAL customer raise their hand — and push away everyone else (unqualified clicks cost the client money).

**3 identifier types:**
1. **Direct** — naming them: "Agency owners," "Health practitioners." Effective but overused; never lazy line-by-line ("Are you a copywriter who wants…").
2. **Shared-Experience / Relatable** — a niche is not a demographic; it's a group sharing the same pains, problems, and goals. "Tell me you're a copywriter without telling me you're a copywriter": nightmare clients, writer's block, "can you write some free emails first?", feast-and-famine. Includes wants, dreams, pains, fears.
3. **Buzz-Word** — niche lingo that grabs insiders without emotion: for chess, "Queen's Gambit," "En Passant"; for copywriters, "CTR," "controls," "retainers." A hook can identify without ever naming the niche.

The same benefit re-skinned per market: "Make money" (bizop) = "Close clients that pay premium" (copywriters) = "Increase your MRR" (SaaS) = "Get more patients" (clinics).

Editing questions (ask aggressively): "Why the HECK does anyone in my niche care about this?" "SO WHAT?" "What's the damn point?"

## Systems 4–6 — The 7 Leads

The lead extends the hook: it converts attention into interest and pulls readers into the body. The prospect's subconscious runs four questions that map to AIDA:
- "What is this? Is it for me?" → **Attention** (hook)
- "Why should I keep reading? I'm busy." → **Interest** (lead)
- "So what? Why does this matter to ME?" → **Desire** (body)
- "Okay I'm in — what do I do?" → **Action** (close)

**Never "assume readership."** The biggest lead mistake is assuming people will read. Edit ruthlessly.

### Lead 1 — Problem-Solution Lead
Describe the reader's problem better than they can → they assume you have the solution. Dig into what keeps them up at night, then directly promise the solution if they keep reading. Ideal energy: hopeful and light (heavy/doom energy kills most markets).

### Lead 2 — The Big Secret Lead (MV's favorite)
Tease a solution beyond anything they've seen; exciting energy; the secret must feel easy and right under their nose. Two flavors:
- **Secrets with a new name** — "Curiously Familiar" naming: familiar enough to feel real, novel enough to itch ("Digital Real Estate," "Lead Flipping," "Amazon Digital"). Caution: constantly renaming erodes a personal brand's authority; save for launches/front-ends.
- **Secrets without a name (tease benefits)** — "the ONE thing between you and your dream weight," "little-known trick to double your productivity."

### Lead 3 — Promise Lead
Go straight to benefit copy from the first line. Strengthen with **Benefit Amplifiers**:
- **Timeframe compression** — "explained in 6 minutes and 24 seconds"
- **WITHOUT X** — the result minus the thing they hate ("without cold outreach," "without supplements")
- **Credibility boosters** — authority, proof numbers, testimonials + guarantees woven into the promise

### Lead 4 — Offer Lead
Direct pitch. Only works on the ~3% already in buying mode (promos, or offer-aware traffic). Two-step build:
1. List the most emotionally compelling parts of the offer (opportunity, accessibility, "even if…", best benefit without worst nightmare).
2. Compress into a clear marketing message with **NRO** (see Part B).

### Lead 5 — Proclamation / Event Lead
Lead with something bold that HAPPENED (news, platform changes, celebrity/guru moves, studies). Steps: (1) state the bold event — if it isn't bold, make it bold; (2) back it with authority (statistics, reports, screenshots, third parties); (3) sneak in promises/implied benefits.

### Lead 6 — Story Lead (MV's second favorite; first choice when time allows)
**No throat clearing** ("Today I want to share a story…" says nothing — kill it). Two entry techniques:
- **Middle of the action** — open on dialogue or crisis: "**Card declined.** / 'Sorry, let me try this one.' / **Card declined.**"
- **Time and place** — the safer entry: "Back in 2016, I burned out on the launch model. Hard." (Place can be situational, not physical.)
Stories are drama: humans doing human things, specific and vivid, layers peeled slowly.

### Lead 7 — Burning-Question Lead
Open with the exact question/objection in the prospect's head as dialogue — for aware, skeptical audiences. Not the lazy "So many people ask me…" — frame it as their own inner voice or a confrontation ("'If your face isn't looking at your computer screen, then you're broke!' / Do you know how tired I am of hearing this CRAP?").

## Systems 7–9 — Credibility (Proof Architecture)

Modern buyers are maximally skeptical; bold promises get rejected even when true. **Prove BEFORE you claim** — establish credibility first so claims land on trust; never claim first and defend after (that's pushing, and pushing loses).

**Level model:** What you say (level 1) < what others say (level 2) < what prospects say to THEMSELVES (level 3 — the ultimate).

### Your Words (System 7)
Credibility from the writer/brand's own record: what you've done, accomplished, results created, stories that demonstrate character, guarantees, reason-why logic. Weave it, don't dump it: "After almost 20 years in business… (at least until you're at about $3M)" — the parenthetical implies the achievement without stating it.

### Other People's Words (System 8)
1. **Ideal customer reviews/testimonials** — collect with the 3-question ask: What was it like before? What's it like now + the result? What would you say to someone on the fence? Mine testimonials for identifier language ("chalky aftertaste," "look forward to my shake"). With permission, polish/prompt testimonials for specificity ("Would it be fair to say you tripled your income?" → "More like 2.5x — but definitely working less.") — always accurate, always approved.
2. **Official aggregate reviews** — Trustpilot, Google, Amazon.
3. **Authority reviews/endorsements** — add **bracket boosters** that big-up the endorser: "— Gary Bencivenga (universally considered the world's greatest living copywriter)."
4. **Branding through association** — Forbes, INC., "as seen in," award badges.

### Visual Credibility (System 9)
Intentional visual signals of results and status (environment, lifestyle, social proof imagery, meetup photos, screenshots of wins). In copy terms: reference and place screenshots/receipts deliberately; the receipt IS the argument.

# PART B — CROSS-CUTTING MV FRAMEWORKS

## NRO — Niche · Result · Offer
MV's master marketing-message formula, used "in every single piece of copy":
**Promise to help [NICHE] get [specific dream RESULT] through [OFFER].**
Example build: Copywriters (N) who want to master copy without spending a fortune on gurus (R) via a $2/day program taught by the coach 6-figure copywriters call "The Copy Sensei" (O) → "Learn copy from me for $2/day."
Before drafting anything, the NRO must be stateable in one clean sentence.

## Question-Based Copywriting (QBC)
MV's drafting and editing engine: prompt yourself the way you'd prompt an AI. Draft, structure, and edit by relentlessly asking: "Why does this matter?" "What am I actually saying?" "Can this be shorter?" "So what?" "How can this section be better?" Great copy chiefs critique with questions; internalize the interrogation.

## The 7/10 Consistency Doctrine
Aim for consistent 7/10 copy, not occasional 9/10. Consistency + reliability + appetite to improve beats streaky brilliance ("The former gets fired, the latter gets PROMOTED"). Even A-listers strike out half the time. Practically: draft to 6/10, edit to 7/10, sometimes reach 8/10. Never block on perfection.

## Natural Urgency (vs. fake urgency)
Urgency must be REAL: actual price increases with dates, actual seat caps with current counts ("98/100"), actual door-close deadlines, first-mover advantage on real trends. Never fake countdown timers. The ~7% of the market that spends most of the money responds to genuine scarcity.

## Qualification & Repulsion
Openly disqualify wrong-fit buyers: "Active copywriters with clients only," "We don't work with beginners," "Once in, once out — cancel and you can never rejoin," "If $X is a financial risk for you, this isn't for you." Repulsion raises perceived value, pre-filters buyers, and is honest.

## Cost-of-Inaction Math
Make staying still expensive with explicit arithmetic: monthly gap × 12 = annual bill for not acting; extrapolate to multi-year and lifetime figures; compare to losses that would devastate them ("You'd go into a depression if you lost $100k… but you're PAYING more than that by wasting your potential.").

## The Receipts Doctrine
Proof avalanche > polished claims. Stack named, specific, verifiable wins (names, months, exact dollar amounts, screenshots), including imperfect ones ("The money isn't all in the bank yet — I made these intros in DECEMBER") — the imperfection increases believability. Acknowledge the 4% who haven't succeeded; it strengthens the 96%.

## Environment & Osmosis (Core Belief System)
Recurring persuasion themes that power his angles: you win through proximity to winners; relationships and referrals beat cold outreach (90% of gigs never posted); your network is leverage; a rising tide lifts all ships; the industry moves in fast trend cycles and being in the right rooms is the only reliable early-warning system; pay the price of success (time, money, ego, comfort).

## System 61 — The Noteworthy Format (content-promo emails & organic posts)

The workhorse format for promoting FREE content (videos, interviews, trainings, posts) — the thing shared must genuinely be worth sharing. MV's claim: if a Noteworthy email flops, it's ~80% because the content it points to is weak, not the email. (His diplomatic client version: "Let's compare this data to the views on the videos we're promoting and see if we can LEARN SOMETHING.")

**The flow:**
1. **Intriguing compelling lead** — with an implicit promise: "I'm going to give you something you want, free."
2. **Context** — back up the promise with context they care about (often a personal story beat or dialogue: "'Dan, you're kidding me! No one would EVER pay me that much!'")
3. **Build up desire** — universalize the problem ("a challenge almost any copywriter can relate to"), then tease: "There's one simple way to fix it forever."
4. **Share the free thing + ask for action** (optional image/thumbnail)
5. **Sell it with bullets** — curiosity bullets: "How moving my BED helped me make 6-figures…"
6. **Free thing + CTA again**
7. **Optional P.S. to build more desire** ("If you haven't done these things yet… you can forget becoming financially successful!")

**Variations & craft rules:**
- **Shorten the formula when the hook is EXTREMELY curious** — hook → "Don't believe me, watch this video" → bullets → CTA.
- **Interview promos:** stack 🔥 curiosity bullets WITH timestamps ("go to 14:15") — and deliberately withhold one timestamp: "actually — you know what? No. It's too good. EARN the answer by watching the video."
- **Anecdotal-lead variant for social:** open with an urgent claim + fresh observation ("Today while looking for inspiration on YT, I saw a video with almost 2m views about 'how to become a copywriter'"), agitate the industry shift, segment the reader ("If you've been in the game a while… / And if you're relatively new…"), then present the interview as the answer. Disclose honestly: "There is no upsell or pitch of any kind in this interview."
- **Ghostwriting-grade example structure (Dan Martell):** fresh personal anecdote → hard truth ("You can't outgrow a bad product") → vivid metaphor (leaking arteries) → video as the surgical tools → bullets → engagement CTA ("Let me know in the comments your biggest takeaway") → branded sign-off nickname ("Dan 'The SaaS Surgeon' Martell").
- **Rule-breaking clause:** when the audience is copywriters (they can see the formula), deliberately break/remix the structure to stay unpredictable. For every other audience, run the standard formula straight.

## System 76 — Urgency & Reminder Emails

Most copywriters do urgency wrong: countdown timers, "LAST CHANCE!", exclamation stacks, ALL CAPS — which reads desperate. Real urgency makes people feel they're genuinely missing out.

**Formula #1 — Reminder Email** (mid-promo):
1. Intriguing lead
2. Opportunity / thing happening ("I didn't want you to miss this…")
3. Marketing message (NRO)
4. Reminder CTA
5. Benefits in bullets (3 or so, "How to…" framed, WITHOUT-X loaded)
6. Inject urgency (limited seats, closing time)
7. Urgency CTA
Then P.S. restates the mechanism + proof + final CTA. Countdown GIFs work well for live-training reminders ("feels like a movie and a timer at the same time").

**Formula #2 — Urgency Email** (final 1–2 emails of a sequence; pushes indecisive buyers over the edge):
1. Time / spots running out (open with the deadline fact)
2. Marketing message
3. Benefits
4. Scarcity CTA
5. Benefits reframed a different way
6. Scarcity CTA
7. **Take-away selling** (what happens after the door shuts; "once we start, you can't join")

**MV's urgency craft rules (from his examples & commentary):**
- **Reason-why urgency.** The scarcity always has a believable, often mundane backstory: a warehouse delivery mix-up, a stock delay that pushed a sale late, daylight savings adding an hour. The mundane reason IS the believability.
- **Casual-life texture inside deadline emails:** "Gotta keep this short as I'm lending a hand in our warehouse," "We're off to dinner and then to get ready…", "I'll be sleeping… then I'll ice bath and do a mini workout before I shut it down." The seller has a life; the deadline is real, not theatrical.
- **Human deadline honesty** beats precision: "I'm supposed to take it down at midnight but realistically it's when I wake up" — this *increases* urgency because it's unpredictable.
- **Creative urgency mechanics:** entries-per-$75 contest structures (urgency + launch in one), reverse-exclusivity ("A-List copywriters are BANNED from subscribing this month" — scarcity via who's *excluded*), "bad news" framing for sale endings.
- **Short-n-sweet apology variant:** "I'm so sorry, [FirstName]…" → deal ends MIDNIGHT → "I wouldn't dilly-dally… not sure there's much stock left" → single CTA → P.S. that the door may close EARLY.
- **Regret-math story variant** (Ian Stanley pattern MV endorses): personal story of an investment not taken (Bitcoin at $300) → "you'll never regret investing in skill building" → tie to offer → honest, casual deadline.
- Mix reminder/urgency emails in toward the END of a promo, after regular direct promo emails.

## The From-Scratch Roadmap (client acquisition & career system)

From his teaching content — MV's step-by-step for building a copywriting business from $0 (also prescribed to $3–10k/mo writers). This is the strategic worldview behind his nurture content:

1. **Interview the industry first.** Talk to 10–20 copywriters currently making good money (15–20 min chats; offer to pay only if ignored). Find where the deal flow actually is — if 7 of 10 say creative strategy, that's the market. Applies fractally: "How do you close clients?" → ask 10 people successfully doing it.
2. **"Who do I need to know?"** Build the core network in the chosen market: add, follow, engage, immerse. Don't hide among peers at your level — get proximate to winners. Entry frame is honest curiosity, not faking success: "I'm here, I'm learning; here's what I built before, now I'm breaking into this."
3. **Personal brand = access, not sales.** The brand exists so industry people take you seriously and connect — professional photos (good lighting, never night shots, not your brother with a phone), interesting background, frequent FB posts telling your story and what you're learning (tag people you spoke with), CV + mini portfolio of 5–10 spec concepts in the niche. "Use social media as business media" — friends and family go on WhatsApp.
4. **Approach clients consistently.** Whatever channel the research revealed (applications, LinkedIn, cold email), follow it consistently. Pay-to-play is legitimate: buy your way into the right rooms if you can. **"If something's not working, optimize the system. Don't change the strategy."**
5. **Mindset = pressure tolerance, not affirmations.** Beliefs matter *after* you know what to do. The real questions: can you handle rejection, failure, disappointment, and still take action? (Ronaldo taking the final penalty.) Self-worth stories are "not appropriate in a business context" — park them, resolve them elsewhere, keep taking action.
6. **Deliver the best copy possible.** Get good, get feedback, take pride in the craft. Retention comes from results.
7. **Be a pleasure to work with + charge reasonably.** Great work is the *expectation*; being enjoyable in meetings is the differentiator. "The best people in this industry are the likable ones."

Supporting metaphors he reaches for: without a destination, no wind is favorable; racing barefoot vs. having the right gear; marathon not sprint.

## Consistency Doctrine & Client-Getting Hierarchy

- **"Consistency beats strategy every single time."** Dramatized with pseudo-math (1/10 strategy × 9/10 consistency beats the reverse) and the Hormozi line he cites: "do so much volume it would be unreasonable for you to fail." The 90-days-of-nothing-then-3-deals mentee story is his proof archetype.
- **Current client-getting rankings (his 2025–26 teaching):** Referrals/intros via network > applications > cold outreach (which he now actively does NOT recommend, while honoring that it worked in older stories). Applications doctrine: the job post literally TELLS you they're hiring, what they need to hear, and what to offer — "the easiest client getting strategy in the game"; the only hard part is tailoring your experience to the client.
- **Networking prescription:** two 15-minute connection calls per week as a non-negotiable. Reach-out method: shared community > shared interest > screenshot something specific from their content and open with it ("Put a bit of thought and effort in!"). Etiquette: never turn a connection call into a pitch — schedule a separate call if business comes up. Underlying beliefs: "People are a lot more friendly than you think," most brands hire several creative strategists at once so friends pull friends onto accounts, and the intro chain compounds (one client → intro → intro → "1/6th of the Dojo works with the top two brands in the industry").
- **Anti-AI-slop stance ("the trust recession"):** audiences are calling out obvious AI content; trust is a bank account — helpful content deposits, slop withdraws; readers may not name what's wrong but FEEL it (car-making-a-funny-noise metaphor); "if you're not willing to invest your time to write to them, why should they invest their time to read it?" Nuance: he's not anti-AI-leverage, he's anti-deception — relevant guardrail for a brand whose copy is produced with AI assistance: output must meet his human-effort quality bar.

## Two-Paths Close
Standard closing structure: Path #1 = stay where you are (painted vividly with ongoing costs and envy) vs. Path #2 = act (painted with capability, confidence, income). "The choice is yours." Then deadline restated.
